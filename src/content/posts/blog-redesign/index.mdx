My blog used to be minimal on purpose. It used system fonts, a tag cloud that had gotten a bit busy, and a header with my photo that was supposed to look kind of like a social profile. I never quite liked how the header turned out. With the blog becoming more developer-focused, I wanted to rethink the design.

AI is part of how I work now, but a lot of people are cranking out boring, generic slop with it. I wanted this redesign to **draw from classic print design and have more character**. Before I asked for a mockup, I gave the agent photos of magazine pages and a book cover, as well as links to several sites and blogs that I liked.

I’ve had those magazines since I was a teenager. The book is, of all things, about _Putin_. I spotted it in a bookstore and liked the cover design.

I often snap pictures of books, posters, billboards, album covers, movie titles, or anything else I like that I can draw inspiration from later.

I wanted to emulate **the typography and funky layouts from the late ’90s music magazines**: big condensed headlines, halftone photos, small type packed in around big type. And I decided to keep my red-and-black color scheme.

## Three directions

I used Grok Bot for much of the initial design work. I hadn't used it for design before. It was surprisingly good and pretty fast too. After I gave it the references and answered a series of questions, it built three homepage mockups using my real posts.

- **Quiet:** a narrow column, one featured post, and an archive grouped by year.
- **Editorial:** a black nameplate, a large featured post with three cards under it, and mostly full-color images.
- **Loud:** a huge red SOARDS nameplate, a tall red-and-black halftone cover image, an all-caps cover story, and a grid of red thumbnails.

All three were pretty good. Minimal was another style I was considering, and the editorial version was a nice middle ground. The loud version was fully inspired by music magazines, and it was my favorite. **I wasn't trying to play it safe on a personal site.**

Two details came directly from the reference photos. The red-and-black halftone on the post thumbnails came from the Putin book cover. The monospaced captions, with their runs of forward slashes (`//` and `////`), came from the geeks article.

## Losing the impact, then getting it back

At first I thought the masthead and cover headline of the loud version took up too much space above the fold, so I changed the layout to be more like the editorial version and moved the editor's letter above the cover story. I switched the headings to lowercase Anton and picked Literata for the body text instead of system fonts. After a few rounds, the mockup looked like this:

I attempted to have Grok Bot kick off a cloud agent in Cursor to implement the code, but that didn't work out too well. It overcomplicated everything and kept stopping on every turn. Eventually I started over in Codex with GPT-6.1-Sol and a fresh prompt.

Once the initial implementation was finished, I returned to Cursor in order to use Opus 5.5 for revisions. It was at this point I decided that **we had lost some impact compared to the original loud layout**. I brought back the large nameplate, with a small italic first name above it.

The top right corner was mostly empty, and that's prime screen real estate, so I added an "Inside this issue" section with a list of recent post titles.

This version was a lot closer to the original loud mockup than the one I'd shrunk it down to. The nameplate looked more like a logo, and **the latest post got the full cover story treatment** with a large image and headline.

I decided to use all lowercase headlines instead of all caps like most of the references. And I liked the warm paper background in the original mockups, but ultimately went with pure white to take full advantage of <a href="https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Radix Colors</a>. Its text and background colors are designed to work together for accessible contrast.

## Before and after

The old site and the redesign use the same posts, but the new layout gives them a different presence. The latest post gets a cover story, more stories appear in the cover lines and image grid, and the older posts stay in a compact archive.

## Gather your own references

Collect inspiration from wherever you find it, in print or on the web. **Pick out what you like from different sources and combine those ideas into something new.** The magazines and book cover were my starting point for this site. Yours could look completely different.
